# Full Set of 20 Directives

The five layers in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) organize these 20 source directives. When a particular instruction is ineffective, use this reference to find alternate wording.
Each entry follows the order **source directive / effect / positive alternative / prompt wording**.

## L1 - Source: Who Took It, and Why?

### 1. Declare the Photographer and Medium

- **Source directive**: Complete this as a natural smartphone photo of an adult Korean, Japanese, or American woman in her twenties, like someone who might actually appear on social media.
- **Effect**: This directive alone improves realism by interrupting the pull toward advertising and editorial photography early in the prompt.
- **Prompt wording**: A natural photo of a woman in her twenties, taken on a smartphone, the kind that would actually exist on someone's social media.

### 4. Distinguish the Purpose From Advertising

- **Source directive**: Make it look like an everyday photo taken by an ordinary person, not an advertising-model shoot.
- **Effect**: Produces the appeal of a plausible nonprofessional subject.
- **Positive alternative**: A frame casually captured by a friend.
- **Prompt wording**: Not an ad shoot; a snapshot an ordinary person took during an ordinary day.

### 5. State the Overall Realism Goal

- **Source directive**: Avoid elements that look AI-generated and create a single frame that could plausibly be posted to a real social-media account.
- **Effect**: Suppresses the overall generated look.
- **Caution**: This is a broad goal and is weak on its own. Pair it with the concrete directives below.
- **Prompt wording**: Avoid anything that reads as AI-generated; make it a single frame that could be posted to a real feed.

### 6. Specify the Capture Device

- **Source directive**: Use the texture of an everyday smartphone snapshot rather than a high-end camera.
- **Effect**: Prevents excessive cleanliness and improves realism.
- **Caution**: Listing camera specifications such as 85 mm, f/1.4, and ISO values pulls the result back toward editorial photography.
- **Prompt wording**: The texture of a casual phone snapshot, not a high-end camera.

## L2 - Light and Image Quality

### 7. Restrict the Light Sources

- **Source directive**: Make it look lit only by natural window light or the lights already present in the location.
- **Effect**: Light blends naturally, shadows fall in coherent directions, and color temperatures can mix.
- **Prompt wording**: Lit only by window light or whatever lamps happen to be in the room, with no studio lighting.

### 2. Suppress Synthetic Surfaces

- **Source directive**: Suppress CG-like surfaces, excessive beautification, and unnatural sheen to create a sense of physical presence.
- **Effect**: Prevents smooth, synthetic-looking skin.
- **Positive alternative**: The texture of an unfiltered original left as it appeared in the phone gallery.
- **Prompt wording**: No CG-like surface, no heavy beautification, and no unnatural sheen.

### 8. Allow Mild Image-Quality Defects

- **Source directive**: Retain slight handshake blur, smartphone noise, and mildly rough resolution.
- **Effect**: Moderately imperfect image quality moves the result closer to an ordinary photograph.
- **Caution**: Always use degree modifiers such as "slight" and "mild." Without them, the result becomes a failed photograph.
- **Prompt wording**: Keep slight handshake blur, phone-camera noise, and a mild softness in resolution.

## L3 - Composition and Moment

### 9. Make the Framing Slightly Imperfect

- **Source directive**: Use natural framing with a small tilt or uneven negative space instead of refining the composition perfectly.
- **Effect**: Prevents excessive order and makes the image feel human-captured.
- **Positive alternative**: The horizon tilts one or two degrees and the subject sits left of frame.
- **Prompt wording**: Framing slightly off, with a degree or two of tilt and the subject not centered.

### 10. Replace Posing With an Ongoing Action

- **Source directive**: Capture an incidental moment during daily life rather than a pose created for the camera.
- **Effect**: Removes the staged quality and produces more natural appeal.
- **Positive alternative**: Name the middle of an action, such as setting down a cup or brushing hair aside.
- **Prompt wording**: Caught mid-action during an ordinary moment, not posed for the camera.

### 14. Relax the Gaze

- **Source directive**: Use a natural gaze slightly away from the camera instead of fixed, direct eye contact.
- **Effect**: Reduces the staged feeling.
- **Positive alternative**: Looking toward the window, slightly away from the camera.
- **Prompt wording**: Eyes slightly off-camera, not locked on the lens.

## L4 - Anatomy

### 11. Keep Facial Structure Coherent

- **Source directive**: Keep the facial contour, bone structure, eyes, brows, nose, and lips in realistic and coherent proportion.
- **Effect**: Prevents awkward relationships between facial features.
- **Prompt wording**: Realistic, coherent proportions across the jawline, brow, eyes, nose, and lips.

### 12. Constrain the Eyes

- **Source directive**: Avoid oversized pupils and retain a natural amount of sclera, slight left-right variation, and real eyelid thickness.
- **Effect**: Prevents an unnatural generated face.
- **Positive alternative**: Human-sized pupils, with the upper eyelid covering a small part of the iris.
- **Prompt wording**: Normal pupil size, natural sclera, eyelids with real thickness, and slight left-right variation.

### 13. Restrain the Expression

- **Source directive**: Keep smiles understated, using only minute movements at the corners of the mouth or eyes.
- **Effect**: Produces a more human expression.
- **Positive alternative**: An almost neutral expression with only a slight lift at one corner of the mouth.
- **Prompt wording**: Expression barely there, with only a small movement at the corner of the mouth or eyes.

### 15. Add Subtle Asymmetry

- **Source directive**: Retain natural left-right differences in the brows, eyes, and corners of the mouth instead of perfect bilateral symmetry.
- **Effect**: Prevents an overly perfect, doll-like face.
- **Positive alternative**: The left brow sits slightly higher than the right, and one corner of the mouth lifts more. Naming the side is more effective.
- **Prompt wording**: Subtle asymmetry, with one brow a touch higher and one corner of the mouth lifting more.

### 16. Preserve Skin Texture

- **Source directive**: Retain a moderate amount of pores, fine facial hair, faint redness, and uneven natural shading rather than smoothing the skin uniformly.
- **Effect**: Produces human skin texture.
- **Positive alternative**: Faint redness beside the nose and across the cheeks, slight forehead shine, and visible pores.
- **Caution**: This directive can increase perceived age. When age matters, add an age lock and use shine, pores, or acne marks instead of wrinkles for subjects in their twenties and thirties.
- **Prompt wording**: Visible pores, fine vellus hair, faint redness around the nose, and uneven natural shading.

### 17. Constrain the Hands

- **Source directive**: Keep hands and fingers plausible in count, joints, length, overlap, and the way they hold a smartphone or another object.
- **Effect**: Reduces malformed hands.
- **Caution**: Hands still fail most often. Excluding them from the frame or partially obscuring them is safer.
- **Caution 2**: Correct anatomy does not guarantee a plausible action. If a hand is doing something, separately describe the hand shape required for that action, such as "the hand holding the garlic keeps its fingers curled back."
- **Prompt wording**: Hands with the correct number of fingers, plausible joints and lengths, and a realistic grip.

### 19. Leave Hair Irregularities

- **Source directive**: Leave flyaways, loose strands, and irregularities in tied hair instead of arranging every strand evenly.
- **Effect**: Adds soft photographic realism.
- **Positive alternative**: A few loose strands across the forehead and side hairs escaping from a tie.
- **Prompt wording**: Hair not combed into even strands, with flyaways, a few loose pieces, and an imperfect tie.

## L5 - Attractiveness Ceiling

### 3. Limit Refinement

- **Source directive**: Preserve attractiveness without perfecting the face or skin.
- **Effect**: Retains human realism.
- **Prompt wording**: Keep the subject attractive, but do not perfect the face or skin.

### 18. Define Plausible Appeal

- **Source directive**: Keep the subject clean, well-kept, and approachable while prioritizing the kind of appeal that could plausibly exist in everyday life.
- **Effect**: Moves the result toward a realistically attractive person.
- **Prompt wording**: Clean, well-kept, and approachable; the kind of good-looking that actually exists.

### 20. State the Final Priority

- **Source directive**: Preserve genuine appeal while prioritizing presence, signs of everyday life, and natural imperfection over perfection.
- **Effect**: Most effective as the final balancing instruction.
- **Caution**: Place it at the end of the prompt. Its position matters because it balances the earlier corrective directives.
- **Caution 2**: Strong beauty language can make the model ignore Directive 16 and return to editorial smoothness. The default should be "preserve a likeable impression." Omit attractiveness language for older adults, documentary scenes, and object studies.
- **Prompt wording**: Keep the subject genuinely attractive, but prioritize presence, lived-in texture, and natural imperfection over perfection.

## Minimum Set by Shot Type

| Situation | Include |
| --- | --- |
| Full body or distant shot with a small face | 1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 20 |
| Upper body with a medium-sized face | Previous set plus 13, 14, 16, 19, 3 |
| Facial close-up | All directives, especially 12, 15, and 16 |
| Hands prominent in frame | Directive 17 is required; partially obscure the hands when possible |
| Selfie | 1, 6, 7, 8, 17 for the phone grip, 13, 16, 20, plus the extended-arm angle and wide-angle distortion |
| Multiple people | 1, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, plus role-based gaze, clear background faces, and clothing variation. Omit 14 because it assumes one subject. |
